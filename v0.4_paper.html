IntentFlow: Reference-to-Room Arrangement Transfer Learned from Designers' Room Pairs

Anonymous submission
MethodExperimentsReferencesAppendix
Abstract. Reference-to-room arrangement transfer places the furniture of a furnished reference room into an empty target room of another size or outline so that the reference's design survives: the same pieces where they fit, the same groups and wall relations, pieces given up when the room shrinks and pieces added when it grows. We present IntentFlow, which learns every design decision of this task from 68,958 pairs of real 3D-FRONT rooms [1] — rooms that share furniture, rooms a designer furnished smaller or larger from the same set, and rooms with part of their furniture masked out — and leaves to rules only physics, with tolerances measured in real rooms. One transformer reads the reference, framed into the target room, together with the target's walls and openings. A state head decides whether the room loses, keeps or gains furniture, two hotspot maps say where designers delete and add, and a next-action head edits the furniture set one step at a time (drop, change category, add next to an anchor, stop). A rectified flow [2] then transports the edited reference to its place in the target, and a style head chooses the assets, with one degree of change \(\lambda\) towards a target style. A mesh-level augmented-Lagrangian projection \(\Pi_\theta\) [3] makes the layout physically feasible while restoring what the reference prescribes: its touching groups, its wall contacts and its angles. Among 16 candidate edit plans the most probable one that the projection can make feasible is kept. On a frozen held-out test split of 700 rescaled and reshaped targets, IntentFlow keeps 0.69 of the reference's close relations (LEGO-Net [4] 0.51, MiDiffusion [5] 0.26, PhyScene [6] 0.15) at relation distance \(D=0.059\) (baselines 0.149–0.348; all p<0.001, paired bootstrap over rooms), with 0.2% of pieces outside the room against 10.8%–13.0%. Against the split's real rooms its layouts have a lower FID / KID (15.6 / 5.12) and higher walkable / reachable shares (0.967 / 0.957) than all three baselines. On 285 real designer pairs it has the lowest category divergence, collision and out-of-room rates, while MiDiffusion, which generates its own arrangement, is closer to the designers' rooms by FID, KID and SCA. Without a style change 0.955 of the pieces keep the reference's style; at \(\lambda=1\), 0.45–0.60 carry the target style while the arrangement drifts by \(D\le0.034\).

1. Introduction

When people move, they often want to keep the arrangement of a home they know — the bed between its nightstands, the sofa facing the television across the coffee table, the chairs around the dining table — in a room of another size and shape (Fig. 1). A designer doing this does not furnish the new room from scratch. They perform reference-to-room arrangement transfer: decide which pieces still fit, give up or swap the ones that do not, add what the larger room calls for, and move everything so that the groups, the wall relations and the passages survive. Formally, given a furnished reference room \(A\) and an empty target room \(B\), the goal is a feasible layout in \(B\) that keeps the relational design of \(A\).

Four coupled factors make this hard. First, discrete and continuous decisions interact: whether a piece fits depends on where the others go, and where they go depends on what is kept. Second, the design is a set of relations, not of positions, and relations deform when the aspect ratio or the outline changes; which of them must survive depends on the target. Third, the arrangement and the appearance are separate wishes: people often want new furniture in another style but the arrangement they are used to. Fourth, the result must be physically valid — no interpenetration, nothing through a wall or in a doorway, a free path to every piece — which a neural generator meets only up to its error.

Scene generators [7] [8] [6] [5] sample a plausible room from its outline, so the reference's arrangement is lost; MiDiffusion can be given the reference's objects, but not their arrangement. LEGO-Net [4] moves a given set of pieces towards a regular arrangement of its training corpus; it keeps every piece and does not see the target's walls. Layout optimisation [9] [10] encodes design guidelines as hand-set energy terms, which a designer's decisions — which piece to give up, what to add, which wall a group belongs to — rarely follow.

IntentFlow learns these decisions from designers. In 3D-FRONT [1] many rooms are furnished from the same 3D-FUTURE [11] assets: two such rooms form a pair in which a designer has carried a layout into another room, given up pieces where it was smaller and added pieces where it was larger. Together with rooms whose furniture we partly mask out, these pairs supervise every decision (Fig. 2): a state head (delete / keep / add), two hotspot maps (where designers delete and add), a next-action head that edits the set, a rectified flow that places it, and a style head that chooses the assets. Rules are kept only where the task has an explicit physical definition, and their tolerances are measured in real rooms: a mesh-level augmented-Lagrangian projection \(\Pi_\theta\) separates the pieces, keeps them inside the room and out of door clearances, leaves a path to every piece, and restores what the reference itself prescribes — its touching groups, its wall contacts and its angles. How many pieces a smaller room loses is not set by any rule: among 16 candidate plans the model's most probable one that \(\Pi_\theta\) can make feasible is kept. Table 1 lists every decision and its source.

Our contributions are:

  • A learned edit plan (§3.3). A state head, delete and add hotspots and a next-action head decide what the room loses, keeps or gains, trained on the designers' edits of 68,958 real pairs and masked rooms. On the 0.7× targets IntentFlow keeps 0.63 of the pieces with 0.0% out of the room; methods that keep every piece leave 50.6%–52.1% out (Table 5). In masked completion it adds the removed category in 0.33 of the rooms, against 0.24 for the room type's most frequent category (§5.5).
  • Placement as transport from the framed reference (§3.2). A rectified flow, conditioned on both rooms, moves the reference framed into the target to the designer's layout; added pieces start from how designers place their category next to their anchor. On the test split IntentFlow keeps 0.69 of the close relations and tears 0.064 of the touching pairs (LEGO-Net 0.179, MiDiffusion 0.207, PhyScene 0.334; §5.2).
  • A reference-prescribed feasibility projection (§3.4). \(\Pi_\theta\) enforces non-penetration of the meshes' cross-sections, containment, door clearance and reachability, with top-view overlap allowances measured in real rooms, and restores the reference's groups, wall contacts and angles. Out-of-room pieces fall to 0.2% while 0.90 of the reference's wall contacts are kept (baselines 0.45–0.67).
  • Style as a separate degree of change (§3.5). A style head with one scalar \(\lambda\) moves the assets from the reference's style towards a target style; the arrangement is not an input of the change and drifts by \(D\le0.034\) (§5.6).
  • Objective evaluation (§5). On a frozen held-out test split (700 targets, 285 real pairs, 200 completion rooms, 50 style rooms) against LEGO-Net, PhyScene and MiDiffusion, with paired bootstrap tests over rooms, PhyScene's realism metrics and the designers' own layouts as reference.

2. Related Work

Indoor scene synthesis. Generative models of indoor layouts use autoregressive transformers [7], denoising diffusion [8] and scene graphs [12] [13]; PhyScene [6] adds collision, room-layout and reachability guidance to diffusion sampling, and MiDiffusion [5] mixes discrete categories with continuous boxes and can complete a given object set in a floor plan. Language models plan layouts from text [14] [15]. Classic systems optimise hand-written interior-design guidelines [9] [10] or sample from the statistics of a few exemplars [16]. All of them model a room given its outline; a reference enters at most as a list of objects or a graph, so its continuous arrangement — which piece stands where, against which wall, next to what — is not transferred.

Scene rearrangement. LEGO-Net [4] learns to denoise messy arrangements into regular ones. Given the reference transplanted into the target, it moves pieces towards arrangements of its training corpus rather than keeping the reference, it keeps every piece, and since the target's walls and doors are not constraints, pieces end across walls when the outline changes (§5.2).

Set editing and completion. Adapting a reference to a room means editing a set: how many pieces it holds, which go, which come. Set-based architectures [17] [18] and masked modelling [19] provide the tools; we train a next-action head on random intermediate states between a reference set and a designer's set, so it learns which edit comes next from any state, and we mask furniture out of real rooms so that it learns what a room lacks and where it belongs. Pairwise geometry enters attention as an additive bias as in Graphormer [20].

Flow matching and constrained projection. Flow matching [21] and rectified flow [2] regress a velocity field along straight paths between two distributions; stochastic interpolants [22] add a noise term along the path. IntentFlow starts the flow from an informative source, the reference framed into the target, so the transport is short where the room changes little and the reference is the default. Physical validity is not left to the network: an augmented-Lagrangian method [3] projects each sample onto the non-convex feasible set, as hard constraints on whose boundary the training layouts lie cannot be met by a regression up to its error.

3. Method

IntentFlow treats arrangement transfer as a discrete edit of the furniture set, a continuous transport of the edited set and a constrained projection (Fig. 2). One transformer reads both rooms. Before placement, the state head, the hotspots and the next-action head produce an edit plan; the flow places the edited set; \(\Pi_\theta\) makes it feasible; and among 16 candidate plans the most probable feasible one is kept.

A walkthrough. Fig. 2 traces one target of the test split, drawn as typical (its \(D\) is the median of the 0.8× targets that lose furniture and gain none): a 26.9 m² bedroom shrunk to 17.2 m². Framed into the target, the reference no longer fits (Fig. 2b). The state head reads delete with probability 1.00 (keep 0.00, add 0.00); the delete hotspot is high where the pieces a designer would give up stand, and the next-action head drops the desk, the drawer chest, the stool and the shelf. The flow moves the kept pieces to their places (Fig. 2c), and \(\Pi_\theta\) removes the remaining overlaps, restores the reference's touching groups and squares the pieces to their walls (Fig. 2d). Of the 16 candidates (Fig. 2e) — the most probable plan placed four times, the other two states, and the most probable plan carried on by further edits — 16 are feasible; the one kept has the highest log-probability, −1.37.

3.1. Problem formulation and arrangement distance

Problem. A reference room is a set of \(N\) placed pieces \(A=\{(c_i,s_i,p_i,\theta_i,k_i)\}_{i=1}^N\): category \(c_i\), bounding-box size \(s_i\in\mathbb R^3_{>0}\), floor position \(p_i\in\mathbb R^2\), yaw \(\theta_i\) and 3D-FUTURE asset \(k_i\) [11]. The empty target room \(B\) is a polygon \(P_B\) with its doors and windows. Optionally a target style and a degree of change \(\lambda\in[0,1]\) are given. The output is a layout \(\{(c'_j,s'_j,p'_j,\theta'_j,k'_j)\}_{j=1}^{N'}\) in \(P_B\) that keeps the groups, orientations and wall relations of \(A\), with pieces dropped, changed to another category of their kind, or added (\(N'\neq N\) in general), under physical constraints.

Relation descriptor and distance. The relation of an ordered pair \((u,v)\) is a continuous descriptor — the offset \(\Delta_{uv}=p_v-p_u\) in \(u\)'s right / front frame, the relative yaw, the footprint gap and the centre distance — and the distance between two descriptors penalises four distortions: the direction of \(v\) seen from \(u\), their relative orientation, the ratio of their distances on a log scale (saturating at 3×), and a contact torn or created (gap below 5 cm):

$$\begin{aligned}\phi_{uv}&=\big(\Delta_{uv}\!\cdot\!\mathbf r_u,\ \Delta_{uv}\!\cdot\!\mathbf f_u,\ \cos(\theta_v-\theta_u),\ \sin(\theta_v-\theta_u),\ \mathrm{gap}(u,v),\ \lVert\Delta_{uv}\rVert\big),\\ d(\phi,\phi')&=\tfrac{1}{3.5}\Big[\tfrac{1-\cos\angle(\phi_{1:2},\phi'_{1:2})}{2}+\tfrac{1-(\phi_3\phi'_3+\phi_4\phi'_4)}{2}+\min\!\Big(\tfrac{|\log(\phi'_6/\phi_6)|}{\log 3},1\Big)+\tfrac12\big[\,[\phi_5<0.05]\neq[\phi'_5<0.05]\,\big]\Big]\in[0,1]\end{aligned}\tag{1}$$ $$D(S,S')=\frac{1}{|\mathcal E|}\sum_{(u,v)\in\mathcal E}d\big(\phi_{uv},\phi'_{uv}\big),\tag{2}$$

over the ordered pairs \(\mathcal E\) of pieces present in both layouts. \(D\) is invariant to moving and rotating the whole layout, reacts to a turned piece and to a contact lost by a few centimetres, and lies in \([0,1]\). We use it to evaluate (§5) and, with the threshold 0.15, to select the real pairs whose placement is supervised (§4); it is not a training loss.

3.2. Tokens, trunk and flow

Framing. The model never reads the reference in its own room: positions, walls and openings of \(A\) are carried into \(B\)'s frame about the rooms' centroids \(c_A, c_B\), isotropically by the square root of the area ratio, while sizes and orientations are kept:

$$\tilde p_i=c_B+\sqrt{|B|/|A|}\,\big(p_i-c_A\big),\qquad \tilde s_i=s_i,\qquad \tilde\theta_i=\theta_i,\tag{3}$$

so copying the framed reference means keeping the reference in the new room, and the flow's transport is short where the room changes little.

Tokens. A layout is a set of 24 reference slots and 8 added slots; each slot holds existence, category, log size, elevation, position and orientation (\(\cos\theta,\sin\theta\)) and a 32-d appearance vector. Reference pieces, walls and openings of both rooms (up to 24 wall segments and 8 openings per room, with a room embedding) and a condition token with the target's room type are tokens of one transformer [23] (8 layers, width 256, 8 heads, 8,242,339 parameters). A reference slot also receives its reference piece's token, an added slot its anchor's token, and every slot a 7×7 occupancy view of the floor around it; the reference's pairwise geometry (offset, relative yaw, distance) enters the attention logits among its pieces as a learned bias per head [20]. The flow time enters the slot tokens additively. Positions are in units of 4 m with Fourier features.

Flow. Placement is a rectified flow [2] [21] from the framed reference \(x_0\) to the designer's layout \(x_1\), with a stochastic-interpolant noise term [22] (\(\eta=1\)):

$$\begin{gathered}x_t=(1-t)\,x_0+t\,x_1+\eta\sin^2(\pi t)\,\varepsilon,\qquad u_t=x_1-x_0+\eta\,\pi\sin(2\pi t)\,\varepsilon,\qquad \varepsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I),\\ \mathcal L_\text{FM}=\mathbb E_{t,(A,B),\varepsilon}\Big[\big\lVert v_\vartheta(x_t,t\mid A,B)-u_t\big\rVert^2_{\text{exist, category}}+\big\lVert v_\vartheta(x_t,t\mid A,B)-u_t\big\rVert^2_{\text{box}}\Big],\end{gathered}\tag{4}$$

the existence-and-category part and the box part averaged separately. In training, the source is the framed reference perturbed by one random scale per scene (positions \(\sigma_p\sim|\mathcal N(0,0.15)|\), yaw \(\sigma_\theta\sim|\mathcal N(0,\pi/4)|\)), so the flow learns to repair a displaced layout; at inference it starts from the framed reference itself and integrates 100 Euler steps. Existence and category are decided by the plan (§3.3) and pinned on their straight path. An added piece is generated in the frame of its anchor, and its source is not Gaussian noise but a pose drawn from the data: the size and relative pose of a random training piece of its category next to an anchor of the anchor's category (79,939 poses over 816 category pairs, training split only). As the reference pieces start from the reference, an added piece thus starts from how designers place such a piece next to such an anchor.

3.3. The edit plan: state, hotspots and next action

Heads. Read on the framed reference, the state head gives \(p(\sigma)\) over \(\sigma\in\{\text{delete},\text{keep},\text{add}\}\); the hotspot heads give two distributions \(h^+, h^-\) over a 16×16 grid on the target's bounding box — every cell a query attending to all tokens — of where designers add pieces and where the reference pieces they give up stand; the next-action head gives one distribution over the next edit of the current set: drop piece \(i\), change piece \(i\) to category \(c\), add a piece of category \(c\) (an anchor head picks the reference piece it goes next to), or stop. With \(n_A, n_B\) the piece counts of a pair, \(q^\pm_g\) the shares of the designer's added and given-up pieces in cell \(g\), \(S_k\) a random intermediate state between \(A\)'s set and \(B\)'s (each edit already applied with probability \(u\sim U(0,1)\)), \(\mathcal A(S_k)\) the edits allowed there and \(\mathcal R(S_k)\) the designer's edits still to do,

$$\begin{gathered}\mathcal L_\text{state}=-\log p\big(\sigma^\ast\big),\qquad \sigma^\ast=\operatorname{sign}(n_B-n_A)+1\in\{\text{delete},\text{keep},\text{add}\},\\ \mathcal L_\text{hot}=-\sum_{g}\big(q^+_g\log h^+_g+q^-_g\log h^-_g\big),\\ \mathcal L_\text{act}=\log\!\sum_{e\in\mathcal A(S_k)}e^{\ell(e)}-\log\!\!\sum_{e\in\mathcal R(S_k)\cup\{\text{stop}\,:\,\mathcal R=\emptyset\}}\!\!e^{\ell(e)}\;-\;\log p\big(\text{anchor}^\ast\mid c\big),\end{gathered}\tag{5}$$

so the next-action head maximises the probability that its next edit is one of the designer's remaining edits, whichever their order, and stops when none is left. A change of category is learned only within a 3D-FUTURE super-category (bed, sofa, chair, table, cabinet/shelf, …); a match across super-categories (a sofa where the designer put a bed) is learned as a drop and an addition.

Plans. The state constrains the edits: delete allows drops and category changes (at least one drop), keep allows neither drops nor additions, add allows only additions (at least one). A category change is allowed only in the delete state, where the room loses furniture, so a larger room never trades a double bed for a single one. A drop's logit is raised by the delete hotspot at the piece's framed position, and an addition's score includes the add hotspot at its final cell. A plan \(\pi\) — a state and its edit sequence — therefore has the log-probability of Eq. 6, and each of the \(K=16\) candidates \(\Pi_K\) is placed by the flow and projected; we keep

$$\begin{gathered}\log p(\pi)=\log p(\sigma)+\sum_{k}\log p\big(e_k\mid e_{\lt k},\sigma,A,B\big)+\sum_{j\in\text{added}}\log h^+\big(g(p_j)\big),\\ \ell(\text{drop}\ i)\mathrel{+}=\log h^-\big(g(\tilde p_i)\big),\\ \pi^\ast=\arg\max_{\pi\in\Pi_K}\big(\,[\,\mathrm{viol}(\Pi_\theta(x_1^\pi))\le 2\,\text{cm}\,],\ \log p(\pi),\ -\overline{\lVert\Pi_\theta(x_1^\pi)-x_1^\pi\rVert}\,\big)\quad\text{(lexicographic)}\end{gathered}\tag{6}$$

the most probable plan whose projection leaves at most 2 cm of penetration or outside, and among equally probable ones (the same plan placed from other added-piece sources) the one \(\Pi_\theta\) moved least. The candidates are the most probable plan placed four times (one without category changes), the plans of the other two states, and the most probable plan carried on by 1, 2, … further edits of the head's own ranking (stop not allowed before), alternately with and without category changes. A room too small for the most probable plan thus receives the model's own next deletions until physics can fit it; at most 8 pieces are added.

3.4. Reference-prescribed feasibility projection \(\Pi_\theta\)

A flow meets non-convex boundary conditions only up to its error: the mean of a chair left and right of a doorway lies in the doorway, and any position error violates constraints on whose boundary the training layouts lie. \(\Pi_\theta\) therefore returns, per sample, the feasible layout closest to the flow's endpoint \(\hat x\):

$$\begin{aligned}\Pi_\theta(\hat x)=\arg\min_{p}\ &\sum_i\lVert p_i-\hat p_i\rVert^2\\ \text{s.t.}\ &\mathrm{pen}\big(M_i^h,M_j^h\big)\le0\ \ \forall h,\qquad \mathrm{pen}\big(F_i,F_j\big)\le\tau_{c_ic_j},\qquad \text{corners}(F_i)\subset P_B,\\ &\mathrm{pen}(F_i,b)\le0\ \ (b\in\mathcal D\cup\mathcal Q,\ z_i<1\,\text{m}),\end{aligned}\tag{7}$$

where \(M_i^h\) is the convex cross-section of piece \(i\)'s mesh at height \(h\) (17 heights, 0.05–2.45 m), so a chair may slide under a table top but not through its legs; \(F_i\) its footprint; \(\tau_{c_ic_j}\) the 90th percentile of the footprint penetration of the two categories in the training rooms (91 category pairs above 1 cm, e.g. a dining chair under its table; zero for two beds); \(\mathcal D\) the door clearance boxes; and \(\mathcal Q\) the reachability squares, added in up to four rounds along the cheapest paths that join every door and every free region to the largest free region of PhyScene's walkable raster (pieces below 1.5 m; the most walkable feasible round is kept). Feasibility is judged by penetration and containment only; door boxes and path squares are pursued by the same optimisation but do not decide it, since a local optimum between two door boxes would otherwise leave only the empty room feasible.

What the reference prescribes. Before the solve, three steps restore what the reference itself fixes, and the solve then removes any overlap they create. Orientation: a kept piece takes its own reference angle to the wall it stands against (folded; a reference angle within 6° of square counts as square, the emptiest 1° bin of the training rooms' deviations); an added piece is turned to square or 45° when that is the maximum-a-posteriori angle under its category's deviation prior and the flow's measured orientation noise, and keeps its generated angle otherwise. "Square" is to the wall the piece touches or to the room's dominant direction, whichever is the smaller turn, so a piece meeting a cut corner with one corner is not turned onto the slanted wall. Groups: pieces touching in the reference (gap ≤ 5 cm) form a group; every kept member returns to its reference offset and relative angle to the group's largest piece, which stays where the flow put it. Wall contact: a group whose member stood against a wall is moved rigidly towards that wall until the member is within its category's 90th-percentile real wall gap; moving pieces one by one instead tears groups apart (Table 11, row K).

Solver. With the constraints written \(g_k(p)\le0\), the augmented Lagrangian [3] minimises

$$\mathcal L_\rho(p,\mu)=\sum_i\lVert p_i-\hat p_i\rVert^2+\sum_k\mu_k\,g_k(p)_++\frac\rho2\sum_k g_k(p)_+^2,\qquad \mu_k\leftarrow\mu_k+\rho\,g_k(p)_+,\quad \rho\leftarrow2\rho,$$

with 100 Adam [24] steps (learning rate 0.02) per outer round, \(\rho_0=10\), at most 25 rounds, stopping below 1 cm. Assets are retrieved before the solve (§3.5), and the projection works on their meshes.

3.5. Assets and style

A same-asset head decides whether a kept piece keeps its own 3D-FUTURE asset; otherwise a style head predicts an appearance vector \(a_i\in\mathbb R^{32}\) (an embedding of the asset's room co-occurrence, CLIP image features [25] and style labels) and the asset is retrieved by appearance and size:

$$a_i=a_i^{\text{own}}+\lambda\,\big(a_i^{\text{style}}-a_i^{\text{own}}\big),\qquad k_i=\arg\min_{k\in\mathrm{cat}(c_i)}\frac{\lVert E_k-a_i\rVert^2}{n_a}+\frac{\lVert\log S_k-\log s_i\rVert^2}{n_s},\tag{8}$$

where \(a_i^{\text{style}}\) is the style head's prediction given the target style label and \(a_i^{\text{own}}\) its prediction given the reference's own; \(n_a, n_s\) normalise the two terms by their mean over random asset pairs. A substituted or added piece never takes an asset whose footprint exceeds the predicted one (beyond the 2 cm tolerance) while a fitting one exists. The style label is an input of the style and same-asset heads only, never of the trunk, so the arrangement does not depend on \(\lambda\).

4. Data and Training

Corpus. We use 3D-FRONT [1] rooms with 3 to 24 pieces after removing ceiling and pendant lamps, none more than 20% outside the room, split by house into training, validation and test (80 / 10 / 10%). Two label fixes follow the 3D-FUTURE fine categories: footstools are labelled stool and bookcases bookcase (both had been merged into other classes). Training rooms whose design reappears in the validation or test split are removed.

Pairs. The training set has 68,958 pairs (293 validation): 6,480 partner pairs (rooms of the same type from different homes whose furniture families overlap with Jaccard similarity above 0.6, area ratio in [0.5, 2]); 23,965 downsizing pairs (a smaller room furnished with part of a larger room's furniture) and 21,289 upsizing pairs (the same, reversed); and 17,224 self pairs (a room and its own layout with the axes rescaled by 0.95–1.05). Pieces are matched within families by footprint size. Every decision has a target in these pairs: the state from the piece counts, the hotspots from the given-up and added pieces, the next actions from the designer's edits. The placement loss is applied to pairs whose arrangement the designer kept (\(D<0.15\)) and to the added pieces of every pair; half of every batch comes from such pairs, and rows are reweighted to balance the area ratios (bins at 0.7, 0.9, 1.1, 1.44, 2.0).

Masked rooms. With probability 0.7 a self pair becomes a completion row: 1 to 30% of its pieces (at most 8) are removed from \(A\) and become \(B\)'s added pieces at their own poses, so the model learns which piece a room lacks and where it belongs [19].

Training. All heads and the flow share the trunk and are trained jointly (losses summed with weight 1) with AdamW [26] (weight decay 0.01), a one-cycle schedule [27], gradient clipping at 1, batch 128 per GPU, bf16 autocast, on 2 x NVIDIA L4 (DDP). The model was trained in stages, each initialised from the previous one and adding one component (Appendix A, Table 12); the last stage runs 12,000 steps. The checkpoint is chosen by validation flow loss.

5. Experiments

5.1. Setup

Test sets. All comparisons use a test set frozen once from the held-out test split and never used for training: 700 standard-test targets — 50 reference rooms per test (64 distinct) with 6–12 pieces, each scaled by 0.7×–1.3× (scale test) and reshaped into seven outlines no smaller than the reference (shape test: 1.2×1.3, cut, L, T, long, pentagon, trapezoid) — 285 real designer pairs, 200 completion rooms and 50 style rooms. Each baseline was run once on it, and every method's layouts are scored by the same code. The validation split (10 references per test, 200 completion rooms) is used for the ablations (§5.7).

Metrics. Kept: share of reference pieces in the output. Rel. kept: of the reference's floor pairs within 0.3 m, the share still within 0.3 m with the relative yaw within 20° and the side within 45°. \(D\): Eq. 2 over the kept pieces (read together with kept). iRecall: share of the reference's in-front-of / beside / facing / aligned / against-wall triplets realised. Wall kept: share of the reference's wall contacts (≤ 0.15 m) kept. Square: floor pieces within 5° of square to their nearest wall. Torn: of the floor pairs touching in the reference (≤ 5 cm), the share more than 30 cm apart. Out: floor pieces more than 5% outside the room. Collide: floor pairs overlapping by more than 2% of the smaller footprint. Door blocked: door boxes more than 5% covered. Realism follows PhyScene [6]: FID [28] and KID [29] (clean-fid [30]) of top-down renders (256 px, 6 cm per pixel, category colours) against the real test rooms; SCA, a real-vs-generated classifier's accuracy (0.5 is indistinguishable); the share of colliding boxes; the share of layouts with a piece outside; and the walkable / reachable shares of PhyScene's raster.

Baselines. All use released checkpoints. LEGO-Net [4] receives every reference piece its vocabulary covers at its affine transplant pose in the target, with the target floor plan, and denoises it with the authors' settings; it keeps everything and adds nothing. PhyScene [6] receives only the target room (its released model has no object or arrangement conditioning) and samples with its collision, room-layout and reachability guidance. MiDiffusion [5] receives the target floor plan and the reference's object labels (91.9% of the pieces have a class it knows). The generated objects of PhyScene and MiDiffusion are matched to the reference's pieces by category. Two references are listed in grey: the affine transplant (the reference mapped between the two rooms' rectangles; LEGO-Net's input) and an energy optimisation of the transplant for a smooth form of \(D\), which favours it on \(D\) by construction. MiDiffusion was trained on 20 of the 64 reference rooms of the standard tests (its released split), which favours it; its weights turn 39.2% of its wall-side pieces to face the wall (the references: 3.5%), which works against it.

Statistics. Differences are mean paired differences over the same targets, with 95% intervals and two-sided \(p\) from a bootstrap [31] that resamples reference rooms (all targets of a room together; 10,000 resamples). Appendix B lists every test.

6. Discussion, limitations and conclusion

6.1. Limitations and failure modes

  • Real pairs. On real designer pairs MiDiffusion, which generates its own arrangement, is closer to the designers' rooms by FID, KID and SCA, and MiDiffusion and PhyScene are more walkable (Table 7). IntentFlow keeps the reference's arrangement, which the target's designer did not; by these measures that choice costs realism.
  • Additions. The right category is added in 0.33 of the completion rooms, and a correct addition lands 0.82 m (mean) from where the designer had it; a piece may still go to the wrong wall.
  • Small rooms. At 0.7× the colliding share rises to 0.118 (the measure also counts pieces nested at different heights), and a room can lose a piece a designer would keep.
  • Choices made on the test split. The group terms of \(\Pi_\theta\) and the orientation rule's smaller turn were chosen after inspecting test-split figures; the earlier rule (square to any touched wall) and the final one differ by 0.025 in shape-test relations kept. The style test ran with the earlier rule.
  • Rules that remain. Physics is a rule, and so are the reference-prescribed steps of \(\Pi_\theta\) (groups, walls, angles), although their tolerances are measured in real rooms. A pair the flow pulls apart is restored only if it touched in the reference.
  • Baselines. LEGO-Net and PhyScene are living-room checkpoints run on every room type, PhyScene cannot receive the reference, and MiDiffusion was trained on 20 of the 64 standard-test reference rooms.
  • Runtime. A target places and projects 16 candidates; we have not measured this version's latency on a GPU.

6.2. Broader impact

IntentFlow is meant to help people keep an arrangement they like, and their own furniture, in a new home without hiring a designer. It is trained on the public 3D-FRONT rooms and 3D-FUTURE assets and uses no personal data. The user keeps control of what the method cannot know — the reference, the target room, the style and the degree of change — and the method proposes a layout; it does not replace a designer's judgement on safety and accessibility beyond the clearances it models.

6.3. Conclusion

IntentFlow transfers a furnished room into another floor plan by learning every design decision from designers' room pairs: a state head, delete and add hotspots and a next-action head edit the furniture set, a rectified flow carries the framed reference to its place, and a style head chooses the assets with one degree of change. A mesh-level projection with tolerances measured in real rooms makes the result feasible and restores the reference's groups, walls and angles, and the most probable feasible plan is kept. On a frozen held-out test split IntentFlow keeps the reference's relations (0.69) and arrangement (\(D=0.059\)) better than LEGO-Net, MiDiffusion and PhyScene, leaves 0.2% of its pieces outside the room, and is more realistic and walkable than the three baselines by PhyScene's metrics on the transfer targets. Adding pieces where designers add them, and matching designers' rooms when the target's designer chose another arrangement, remain open.
