ReRoom: Topology-Preserving Hierarchical Flow with Differentiable Geometric Projection for 3D Scene Retargeting

How can we achieve topology-preserving and physically-constrained scene retargeting under non-uniform metric deformation — without expensive test-time optimization?

Abstract

Retargeting is about relational topology, not absolute coordinates.

Scene retargeting transplants a reference interior design into a new room boundary. Its essence is preserving design intent — the relational topology of furniture (a dining set stays a dining set, a bed keeps its nightstands) — not reproducing absolute positions. Generative models that integrate in a global coordinate frame tear motifs apart under non-uniform scaling, and the usual remedies are worse than the disease: test-time energy optimization is slow and local-minimum-prone, while hard heuristic snapping breaks the generative manifold and injects secondary collisions.

We cast retargeting as a hierarchical, geometry-conditioned continuous flow on a metric manifold, and prove that a metric-relative reparameterization keeps intra-motif topology invariant under anisotropic deformation. We derive a Jacobian manifold-pullback so that absolute-space physical energies guide the flow inside the relative space. Finally, we lift post-hoc geometric cleanup into an end-to-end differentiable anchored proximal projection \(\Pi_\theta\), realized by \(K\)-step unrolling. The result needs only a single network + a single differentiable projection, reaching the lowest collision rate and highest topology retention in our projection ablation while adding negligible latency — no test-time optimization.

The core storyline

Four steps from dilemma to takeaway.

1

The dilemma

Intent lives in topology, but flows integrate in global coordinates.

Retargeting must preserve the relational graph (design intent), not absolute coordinates. Yet diffusion/flow models integrate poses in a global frame; under non-uniform room scaling the metric distance between motif members scales with the room, so a dining set is pulled apart.

2

Why existing fixes fail

Both standard remedies damage the layout.

Test-time energy optimization (Adam polish / MCMC) is slow — hundreds of ms of iterative descent per layout — and prone to local minima. Hard heuristic snapping aligns to walls but bulldozes the generative manifold: it breaks topology (\(S_\text{rel}\) drops) and introduces secondary collisions (\(R_\text{col}\) rebounds).

3

Our insight & method

Hierarchical topology-preserving flow + a differentiable anchored projection.

Representation: metric-relative hierarchical reparameterization — provably topology-invariant under deformation (Prop. 1). Guidance: Jacobian manifold-pullback so global physical constraints act precisely in the local relative space (Prop. 2). Geometry: post-processing becomes a differentiable proximal layer \(\Pi_\theta\) via \(K\)-step unrolling, with gradients flowing back through it (Prop. 3′).

4

The takeaway

Single network + single differentiable projection.

No test-time optimization. In our projection ablation, \(\Pi_\theta\) attains the lowest collision (1.41%) and highest topology retention (\(S_\text{rel}\!=\!0.70\)), adding only ~78 ms over raw sampling — versus +282 ms for polish, which is worse on both.

Method

A hierarchical metric manifold, with the projection promoted into the model.

Let the intent graph be \(\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})\) (nodes = objects, edges = typed relations with elasticity \(\alpha_{ij}\)); motif structure induces a parent map \(\pi:\text{child}\!\mapsto\!\text{head}\). Each object state is \(x_i\in SE(2)\), normalized to the room's minimum-rotated-rectangle frame. The hierarchical map \(\Phi_\mathcal{G}:x\mapsto z\) keeps heads absolute and expresses children in parent-local metric coordinates:

Prop 1 Metric-stretch invariance of intra-motif topology

Under an anisotropic room scaling \(A=\mathrm{diag}(s_x,s_y)\), the child's metric offset is absorbed entirely by its parent's absolute coordinate — the relative code is scale-free:

$$z_c=\tfrac{1}{\lambda}R(-\theta_{\pi(c)})\big(x_c-x_{\pi(c)}\big),\qquad z_c(A\!\cdot\!P_t)=z_c(P_t)$$

Hence \(\lVert x_c-x_{\pi(c)}\rVert\) is preserved up to the parent's pose: motifs do not disperse when the room stretches. Evidence: a 0.6 m offset yields \(z=0.2\) in both a 3 m and a 4.5 m room; 1.35× motif retention 25→42%; raw \(S_\text{rel}=0.98\) at 1.0×.

Prop 2 Jacobian pullback for absolute-space energy

Sampling is an energy-guided ODE. Feasibility energy \(\Phi\) (wall / collision / boundary) is defined in absolute space, but the flow lives on the reparameterized manifold, so guidance must be pulled back:

$$z\leftarrow z+d\tau\,v_\theta-d\tau\,\mathrm{ramp}(\tau)\,\nabla_z\Phi\big(\Psi_\mathcal{G}(\hat z_1)\big),\qquad \nabla_z\Phi=J_{\Psi_\mathcal{G}}^{\top}\,\nabla_x\Phi$$

The Jacobian \(J_{\Psi_\mathcal{G}}=\partial x/\partial z\) carries a parent-motion term for children — the explicit “move the parent, carry the children” chain rule. Implemented as decode → nudge → encode.

Prop 3′ End-to-end differentiable proximal projection \(\Pi_\theta\) — the core

The geometric regularities (wall-flush, inward facing, Manhattan, non-collision) become a smooth proximal objective anchored to the flow's proposal \(x_0\); the projection is a \(K\)-step unrolled gradient operator:

$$\Pi_\theta(x_0)=\underset{K\text{-step unroll}}{\arg\min}\;\; \underbrace{w_a\lVert x-x_0\rVert^2}_{\text{anchor to proposal}}+w_f E_\text{flush}+w_o E_\text{ortho}+w_c E_\text{col}$$

Because every step is a differentiable tensor op, the flow-matching loss can back-propagate through the projection, so the DiT learns to emit layouts already near \(\Pi_\theta\)'s fixed point (OptNet / implicit-differentiation spirit, realized by explicit unrolling — dependency-free). This yields a self-contained neural generator + convex-style projection system: generative flexibility with geometric rigor.

The anchor term is essential: without it, pure energy descent drifts and tears motifs (\(S_\text{rel}\) collapses to 0.566). It is what makes \(\Pi_\theta\) a projection to the nearest feasible layout rather than a re-synthesis.

Honest scope. \(S_\text{rel}\)/\(R_\text{col}\)/snap are on the projection-ablation set (4 references × 3 sizes); base ODE sampling is ~835 ms/layout (k=1, 50 steps, in-sampling guidance on), which every row shares — the table isolates the projection's marginal cost and quality. This is an internal ablation of projection choices, not a comparison against prior systems.

Positioning

Two components, framed for what they are.

Discrete pruning under extreme scale disparity

avoid“a script deletes chairs with while-loops when the room is small.”

frameA two-stage architecture: (1) semantic topology adaptation — graph-based budgeted structural pruning over the intent graph, a discrete decision; (2) continuous constrained geometric synthesis — the paper's core manifold flow. The stages are decoupled by design; the contribution lives in Stage 2.

Training-pair synthesis without paired data

avoid“we wrote deformation algorithms to make synthetic pairs.”

frameCausal inverse-deformation synthesis with strict quality gates: an inverse-boundary mapping that preserves physical causality and motif rigidity, filtered by four physical gates — area-ratio rejection, out-of-bounds thresholding, inward wall clamping, and baseline-bounded collision auditing. A real cross-pairing set (human designs across rooms) serves as an out-of-distribution test, showing the model is not overfit to the synthesis.
